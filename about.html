About Me

Welcome to my page. I am a Year 3 student at the National University of Singapore (NUS), reading Computer Science with a minor in Interactive Media. My work runs along two tracks that keep converging.

The first is evaluation and retrieval. I care about the unglamorous question of how you actually know a system is good — how you build a benchmark whose rankings survive a re-run, how you ground an answer key in something mechanically decidable instead of another model's opinion, and how many items you need before a leaderboard stops moving. At Trip.com I owned an end-to-end evaluation system for LLM travel agents end to end; alongside it I have built retrieval systems themselves, from a cascaded multimodal review-validation pipeline (Harmonia) to a RAG support-agent backend that refuses rather than fabricates when retrieval comes up empty.

The second is graphics and vision. This started as coursework — real-time rendering in OpenGL, ray tracing, mesh processing and computational geometry in C++, character animation in Blender — and it is the reason I picked up the Interactive Media minor. It has since stopped being coursework: I will be teaching assistant for Computer Graphics in AY26/27 Sem 1.

The two tracks meet in my current research. In the NUS Odyssey Research Programme I work on a long-horizon benchmark for video world models, where the graphics side is what makes the evaluation side possible: by authoring controlled scenes in Unreal Engine 5 with scripted camera trajectories and per-object segmentation, a failure can be attributed to the model rather than to an uncontrolled scene. Building the world is what lets you grade the model that imagines it.
